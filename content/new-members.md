## All about us

Lions clubs operate all over the world. Bodmin Lions is a charitable incorporated organisation that mainly supports the community of Bodmin and the surrounding area. Through Lions Clubs International and our district, we also support regional, national and international causes.

We have been going since 1970, and our members are of all ages and backgrounds, with more on their joining journey.

## When we meet

The whole club meets at the Hole in the Wall pub in Bodmin, in the first-floor meeting room, on the **first Monday of the month at 7:30pm**.

A smaller directors' meeting is held on the third Monday of the month at the same place. Trustees and core members attend, and new members take turns to come along so they can see how the club is run.

## What we do

Our two big fundraising events each year are:

- the [Bodmin Moor Ten Tors walk](/events/ten-tors/) (May)
- the [Father Christmas sleigh](/events/father-christmas-sleigh/) (December)

We also help with plenty of other community events, including the [Bodmin fireworks](/events/fireworks/), [St Piran's Day](/events/st-pirans-day/), Bodmin Carnival, the Christmas lights switch-on, [glasses recycling](/events/glasses-recycling/), Pencarrow Snowdrop Sunday, Children's Hospice South West's Santa Run and Moonlight Memory Walk, summer fun days in Priory Park, and Bodmin Riding and Heritage Day.

We regularly receive [requests for funding](/funding/) from individuals and groups, and the club considers each one on its merits.

Every year the **Sean Whitmore award** goes to three local young people who show talent and dedication in their sport.

## The journey to becoming a Lion

1. Fill in the [membership application form (PDF)](/docs/membership-application-form.pdf) and email it to [bodmin.lions@outlook.com](mailto:bodmin.lions@outlook.com?subject=Membership%20application), or ask us to send you a paper copy.
2. Your application goes to the directors' meeting. We'll arrange to meet you, tell you more, answer your questions and hear why you'd like to become a Lion.
3. Your application then comes back to a main club meeting, where members vote on it.
4. If it's accepted, we'll invite you to three meetings so you can get a feel for what happens and decide whether it's for you. If you don't already have a sponsor, we'll give you one to support you through this stage.
5. At the end of this period you'll be invited to become a member. Our President will swear you in and present your Lions badge and welcome pack. We'll also start a DBS check at this point, and membership depends on it.

## What we ask of you

1. Come to club meetings, and send apologies when you can't.
2. Pay the membership fee of £40 twice a year.
3. Get involved in the club's events and committees through the year.

## What you get from joining

Giving some of your time brings a real sense of purpose, and pride in giving something back to your community and beyond. You'll meet new people and work with other Lions and other organisations. Everyone has a part to play, and you can make a difference.

## Lions terms explained

LCI
: Lions Clubs International. The international headquarters is in Oak Brook, USA, so you'll sometimes hear it called "Oak Brook". About half of our membership fees (dues) go there.

LCIF
: Lions Clubs International Foundation, the international charity arm. LCI and LCIF are run by an international board of volunteers — the international President, past and vice presidents, and international directors elected by clubs and districts around the world — supported by paid staff.

MD (multiple district)
: A large group of clubs, usually covering a country. We're in MD105, which covers the UK, with headquarters in Birmingham. MD105 supports district and club officers, handles insurance and national publicity, collects the other half of our dues, is the UK collection point for spectacle recycling, and passes donations on to LCIF. It is run by the Council of Governors and organises the annual MD convention.

District
: A smaller group of clubs. We're in District 105SW. The District Governor and their team are the first point of contact for club support and organise a district convention each year.

Zone
: A few neighbouring clubs. We're in 105SW Zone B, with Fowey River, St Austell and Wadebridge. Each zone has a zone chair who links the clubs.

Club
: Each club is run by its directors: the President, immediate past President, vice presidents, Secretary, Treasurer and a few ordinary members. All club officers are elected each year by the members.

Tail twister
: Keeps meetings fun and friendly by handing out small fines for reasons best known to themselves. If the club thinks a fine was unfair, it can vote to fine the tail twister instead.

Lion tamer
: Looks after the club's equipment and regalia and helps the President at meetings.

Safety and insurance lead
: Works with the district to make sure the club's events are safe and properly covered.

Membership and welfare officer
: Offers members help and support when they need it.

PRO
: Public relations officer: looks after publicity for the club and its events.
